    Lisa Cirenza

    Lisa Cirenza is a painter in the French Alps. Shaped by her time as a young researcher on the Juneau Icefield, she paints light, water, rock and time (ice, snow, fog, lake and mountains) in encaustic, mixed media, oil and watercolour.

    01Artist statement

    Most people look up and see the Alps. I see a record of time, still being written, and light coaxing new life from every layer.

    For as long as I've painted, my work has been about connection: to each other, to the natural world, and to what lies beneath the surface of what we think we see. For years I painted people: strangers sharing a carriage on the London Underground, refugees, the lives carried inside a face, and the forces that divide us. We have never been so connected on the surface, nor so disconnected underneath. My current work follows those forces into the natural world and turns to light, water, rock and time, the things that change too slowly or too quickly for a glance, and can only be seen by someone who stops to look.

    02A Record of Time

    The Geology of the Invisible

    In my work, wax functions as a geological membrane: it preserves gestures while allowing them to be reopened, scraped back, or transformed.

    My new paintings ask for the same thing the ice asked of me: a few minutes of real attention, in a world that rarely asks for more than a second.

    03The series, layer by layer

    A Record of Time

    My current series gathers work where you can see time on the surface: layers, erosion, melt, flow, shifting light. It grows from my years as a young field researcher on the Juneau Icefield and my grief at watching Alpine glaciers disappear. Its message is hope: what we truly see, we are more likely to protect.

    Ice, glaciers, snow, fog, lake and mountains; alpenglow; rock laid bare as a record of time.

    05Collectors' editions

    Small blocks, held in acrylic

    Small 15 × 20 cm works mounted in thick acrylic blocks, from my archive of digital work. They sell quickly (one went within fifteen minutes of the Salon d'Automne opening) and bring new collectors to my work.

    Each piece is dated twice: the year of the image and the year of the block (for example, “Tube series, 2017 — acrylic block, 2026”).
